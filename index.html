01

What sets n-type or p-type?

Doping or gating sets the majority carrier type—not the metal name. The work-function difference then determines depletion or accumulation near the contact.

02

Why does the Fermi level become flat?

At thermal equilibrium, the contact shares one electron chemical potential. Metal Eꜰ is our zero-energy reference under a reservoir approximation; this does not mean the metal can never change.

03

Which direction does the diagram show?

The in-plane direction, from the contact edge into the remote 2D channel. Band shapes are schematic: no 2D Poisson equation is solved, and bulk depletion formulas are not treated as monolayer results.

Model assumptions and real contacts ＋

Ideal Schottky–Mott alignment is assumed: no interface states, Fermi-level pinning, extra interface dipoles, chemical reactions, image-force lowering, or applied bias. The 2D material data describe free-standing 2H-phase GW calculations. The electronic gap is IP − EA for algebraic consistency, not an optical transition energy. Real values depend on thickness, substrate screening, strain, and the interface.

Playback progress is a teaching sequence, not measurable elapsed time. Band profiles and carrier densities are not calculated self-consistently. A positive majority-carrier barrier does not guarantee rectification. A negative ideal barrier means no positive injection barrier for that carrier, not zero contact resistance. Strong bending may cause inversion.

B = ΦM − ΦS is the signed electron-band shift. In n-type depletion, B = qVbi; in p-type depletion, B = −qVbi, where Vbi is a positive magnitude. The two carrier barriers always satisfy ΦBn + ΦBp = Eg.
