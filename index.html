01

What sets n-type or p-type?

Doping or gating sets the majority carrier type—not the metal name. The work-function difference then determines depletion or accumulation near the contact.

02

Why does the Fermi level become flat?

At thermal equilibrium, the contact shares one electron chemical potential. Metal Eꜰ is our zero-energy reference under a reservoir approximation; this does not mean the metal can never change.

03

Which direction does the diagram show?

The in-plane direction, from the contact edge into the remote 2D channel. Band shapes are schematic: no 2D Poisson equation is solved, and bulk depletion formulas are not treated as monolayer results.

Model assumptions and real contacts ＋

Ideal Schottky–Mott alignment is assumed: no interface states, Fermi-level pinning, extra interface dipoles, chemical reactions, image-force lowering, or applied bias. The 2D catalog uses vacuum-referenced PBE band edges from C2DB. χ = −CBM and the electronic gap is CBM − VBM for algebraic consistency (table rounding: 0.001 eV), not an optical transition energy. PBE often underestimates gaps. Different structure records are distinct phases or predictions; inclusion does not imply experimental synthesis or stability. For Janus and other asymmetric sheets, surface orientation can change the vacuum reference; this simplified model does not resolve side-specific contacts. Real values depend on thickness, substrate screening, strain, and the interface.

Playback progress is a teaching sequence, not measurable elapsed time. Band profiles and carrier densities are not calculated self-consistently. A positive majority-carrier barrier does not guarantee rectification. A negative ideal barrier means no positive injection barrier for that carrier, not zero contact resistance. Strong bending may cause inversion.

B = ΦM − ΦS is the signed electron-band shift. In n-type depletion, B = qVbi; in p-type depletion, B = −qVbi, where Vbi is a positive magnitude. The two carrier barriers always satisfy ΦBn + ΦBp = Eg.

Built-in data

Metal presets use representative polycrystalline work functions from Michaelson. The semiconductor selector uses a bundled C2DB snapshot (19 Oct 2026): 4,262 structure records with PBE vacuum band edges and identifiable layer provenance, including 4,255 monolayers and 7 self-intercalated bilayers. χ = −CBM; Eg = CBM − VBM. The source query returned 4,377 records; 115 with unspecified layer provenance were excluded. The selector shows 3,247 unique compositions; equivalent formulas and structures are combined into one material option. For each available layer count, the preset uses the record with the lowest energy above hull among the included records (ties resolved by structure ID). This selects a representative calculation, not a guarantee of experimental stability or a full comparison of phases. No stability filter is applied. Crystal faces, structure, and environment affect the values.
